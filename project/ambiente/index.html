Ambiente: Smart Thermostat

Ambiente is a wall-mounted smart thermostat for underfloor heating and cooling, fan coil units and timer-controlled systems. I designed its touchscreen interface and companion mobile app, covering eight HVAC configurations with one consistent experience.

Product Design
Embedded UI
Mobile App
Light & Dark Mode

The Challenge

One product had to control very different systems. A home with only underfloor heating needs a simple dial; a building with heating, cooling and fan coils needs modes, fan speeds and humidity. Some installs only switch a timer on and off, others flip a whole building between heating and cooling.

The same logic had to work twice: on a small square wall screen with a four-icon control bar, and in a phone app with light and dark themes, without ever feeling like two different products.

UFH is underfloor heating, UFC underfloor cooling and FCU a fan coil unit. Each configuration shows only the controls its system actually has, on both the wall unit and the app.

Optimizing the Experience

Temperature always comes first: one large number, changed the same way everywhere by scrolling or dragging a slider. Everything else, from mode and fan speed to Eco and frost protection, follows a single pattern: tap the current value to change it.

Instead of eight products, I designed one adaptive layout. Each configuration adds or removes controls from the same home screen, so an installer or homeowner who learns one system already knows them all.

The wall thermostat

A square touchscreen that lives on the wall, used by everyone in the room, often in passing. Every screen had to be readable at a glance from across a room.

The start screen shows the room temperature large and nothing else that is not needed. Tapping it opens a scrolling slider with plus and minus controls; once a new target is set, an estimate such as “26° in 27 min” appears under the temperature so people know when the room will be comfortable.

Each state is designed to be understood without reading. Locked shows a single “Press to unlock” bar, errors blink a warning icon that opens the details, power off leaves only the power button, and frost protection shows an illustrated room heating at a safe minimum.

Schedules use a timetable that reads like a sentence: wake, leave, return and sleep, each with a time and a temperature. Weekdays and weekends can differ, any period opens in a focused editor, and the thermostat learns how quickly the room warms up so its time-to-target estimates improve over time.

Everyday settings sit in a simple grid, while installer options such as system configuration, frost protection, calibration and floor limits live under Advanced Settings, grouped by topic. Values like the Eco offset reuse the same slider as the temperature.

The same start screen grows with the system: humidity and heat/cool mode for underfloor cooling, fan speed and Eco or Comfort for fan coils, chosen by tapping the current value. The timer configuration swaps temperature for on/off control, and mode switch changes the whole system between heating and cooling.

The mobile app

The app brings the thermostat to the phone. It mirrors the wall unit’s logic, then uses the extra space for clearer labels, cards and bottom-sheet drawers.

The same big temperature, the same slider and the same orange accent as the wall unit, in light and dark themes, so moving between the wall and the phone feels like one product.

Four of the eight configurations side by side. The temperature and room name always stay on top, and the cards below change with the system: Power, Eco, Frost and Programme for underfloor heating; Mode and Fan speed for fan coils; a clock, Override and Schedule for timers; and System mode for whole-building changeover.

Every change happens in a bottom-sheet drawer over a blurred home screen, so people never lose their place. Options are large cards with a one-line explanation, such as “Auto: follows demand”, and every drawer ends with the same Cancel and Save pair.

Every screen exists in both themes. The warm off-white light mode suits daytime use, while dark mode matches the wall unit’s black screen; the orange accent and blue cooling colour carry across both.

Schedules switch between heating and cooling, weekdays and weekends, and can be copied to other days. Timer installs get an on/off version of the same timetable. After a change, the home screen shows when the target will be reached, and big decisions like switching the whole system to heating ask for confirmation first.

The Result

One design language now covers eight HVAC configurations on two platforms and in two themes. Installers configure the system once, and the interface shows only what that system can actually do.

Homeowners get the same simple habit everywhere: look at the big number, slide to change it, tap a value to adjust it. Whether they are at the wall or on their phone, the thermostat behaves the same way.
