Scaled Cognition: Webflow Website

I designed and built the Scaled Cognition website in Webflow: a calm, precise site for a company building reliable AI for customer experience, with the blog, research, news and open roles running on the Webflow CMS.

Webflow Development
Website Design
Webflow CMS

The Challenge

Scaled Cognition sells a technical product, the APT-1 model, to enterprise CX leaders. The site had to explain it plainly and feel as reliable as the product, without looking like every other AI company.

The marketing team also publishes posts, research papers, news and new roles every week, and needed to do that on their own, without waiting for a developer.

The Approach

I started from the brand guide: DM Sans, the ink, navy and electric blue palette and warm off-whites instead of flat grey. Every section was designed in Figma as a reusable component, then rebuilt one to one in Webflow, with classes named after the design tokens.

Content that changes often lives in the Webflow CMS. Blog posts, research, news and open roles are collections with their own templates, so the team publishes an item and the homepage, Resources and Careers update on their own.

The homepage leads with one claim, super-reliable intelligence for CX, then the logos of the teams behind it. The navigation, hero and buttons are shared Webflow components used on every page.

A scroll story: the statement stays in place while the product visual changes underneath, built with Webflow interactions instead of custom code.

Industry tabs switch the story between banking, retail, travel and telecom without leaving the page. Each tab is a component with an active state.

News and insights on the homepage come straight from the CMS: the three newest blog posts, research papers or announcements, sorted by date in Webflow.

Resources is a CMS collection page: blog and news lists, filtered by type, and one shared template that every article opens in.

Industry pages reuse the same sections in a different order, so a new market page comes together from parts that already exist.

The website library in Figma: colour tokens, type styles and the button and tab components with their states. The Webflow classes use the same names, so design and build stay in step.

The Result

A site that reads as calm and technical as the product.

The marketing team publishes posts, research, news and roles on its own, and new pages are assembled from existing sections in hours instead of weeks.

What’s next
