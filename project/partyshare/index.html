PartyShare: Event Rental Marketplace

I designed PartyShare, a marketplace where people rent everything an event needs from local vendors: tents, furniture, lighting and décor, booked, delivered and returned in one place.

Marketplace Design
Product Design
Web and Mobile

The Challenge

Party rentals are booked by phone and email, one vendor at a time. A single birthday can mean three suppliers, three deposits and three delivery windows, and nobody sees the whole picture.

PartyShare had to work for two sides at once: renters who want one simple checkout, and vendors who need to run stock, requests and payouts without a back office.

The Approach

I built the product on Metronic, a mature admin and commerce kit, so the budget went into the flows instead of the components. One account can rent and list: a switch moves between the Renter and Vendor dashboards.

The checkout belonged to a milestone that was never funded, so I designed it from the components the product already had. Ordering plays in the hero above: one cart holds items from several vendors, each with its own delivery or pickup, and the deposit is only held on the card. The other flows play below, each one clickable.

Roll-up banners for the launch events, light and dark, with a QR code for early access.

A renter signs up

Onboarding asks only for what the first booking needs: a profile, a delivery address and a payment method, then notifications. The address and card come back at every checkout, so ordering stays three steps.

After that the dashboard holds upcoming bookings, spend, messages and the reviews still to leave. Play it, or click through it.

A vendor sets up the store

Vendors answer the hard questions once: business profile, pickup hours and delivery zones, and the deposit, cleaning and cancellation policies every listing inherits. Identity and payouts go through Stripe.

The store opens on a dashboard of requests, bookings and earnings.

A renter opens a store

Anyone who rents can list. The Vendor switch on the dashboard opens the same account as a store, and Create Listing walks through it one step at a time: the basics and photos, fulfilment, specs, rules and fees, then a review of the listing exactly as renters will see it.

Publish, and the item is live in the inventory next to the rest of the stock. Play it, or click through it.

A vendor manages the order

New requests wait at the top of Bookings with their dates, total and fulfilment. The vendor opens one to check the items, approves or rejects the whole request from Actions, and the booking moves into the list while the renter gets a confirmation.

From there the conversation about delivery happens in Messages, tied to the booking.

The Result

A marketplace that handles the whole rental, not just the listing.

Renters book from several vendors in one checkout, and vendors run requests, inventory and payouts from the same account they rent with.
