Senna: Masterpiece Control Panel

Senna builds high-end LED lights for film and TV. I redesigned the touch-screen interface of the Masterpiece, a 2400W RGBWW light, so a gaffer can set colour, effects and DMX in seconds, in the dark and at arm’s length.

Touch Screen Interface
Visual Design
Hardware UX

The Challenge

Eight modes on a screen nobody could read in the dark

The Masterpiece controls colour temperature, RGBW, HSI, gel filters, XY colour, more than 40 effects and wireless DMX, all from two small touch screens on the back of the light.

The old interface used flat grey buttons, tiny type and thin sliders. On a night shoot, from a ladder or a lift, it was hard to see which mode was active and what value the light was set to.

My approach

Studio hardware you can almost touch

I looked at the equipment people already trust in the dark: mixing desks, synthesizers and broadcast consoles. Real knobs, bevelled keys and segment displays became the visual language of the new panel.

Every mode shares one layout: modes on the left, controls in the middle, large digital readouts on the right and six memory slots at the bottom. Learn CCT once and RGBW, HSI and GEL already feel familiar.

The two main screens: brightness, colour temperature and green and magenta correction on knobs with striped tracks, and an XY chart for matching an exact colour. Every value sits in a large segment readout that stays legible from across the set.

The two touch screens sit on the back of the Masterpiece, next to the power and DMX connectors. HSI mode puts hue, saturation and intensity on the same knobs and readouts.

Every LED channel on its own slider, and LEE and Rosco filter libraries shown as real colour swatches.

More than 40 effects grouped by category, and a DMX screen for the address, channel mode and wireless link quality.

The Result

A consistent, high-contrast interface across all eight modes, designed for the light’s built-in touch screens and readable at a glance on set.
